# Niagara hull verification status

**Current update:** [Pass 4](MODEL-PASS-4.md) replaces the flat stern with an approximate short rounded end and fuller quarters. The following notes retain the research history; measured Steers offsets remain unavailable.

Reviewed 31 October 2026 in response to the user's overhead screenshot.

The current Niagara model is a dimension-controlled approximation, not a traced lines-plan reconstruction. Its half-breadth uses a procedural curve with a deliberately broadened stern transom. That modification removed the earlier second pointed end, but does not establish that the resulting transom breadth, stern corners, quarter curvature or submerged sections match the actual ship. An overhead screenshot of this model cannot prove those features correct.

Period photo references include [NHHC NH 57980](https://www.history.navy.mil/our-collections/photography/numerical-list-of-images/nhhc-series/nh-series/NH-57000/NH-57980.html), showing Niagara circa 1863 after the 1862–63 modifications, and the locally retained NH 75895 broadside photograph. These support the general refit silhouette but do not provide a complete overhead or body-plan geometry.

A potential archival lead was found in the [Bristol Dockyards ship-plan inventory](https://bristoldockyards.cdn.prismic.io/bristoldockyards/aicvCweQX7-eW_JT_SHIP-PLANS-collection-Oct-2024.pdf), printed page 146 (PDF page index 145):

- `2012.05134`: Niagara, sail plan/profile, attributed in the inventory to the Hart Nautical Museum at MIT.
- `2012.05135`: Niagara, lines plan including sheer, half-breadth and body plan.

The inventory entries do not establish the vessel's date or identity. They must not yet be presented as verified plans for the 1855 steam frigate. The drawings themselves have not been obtained, purchased or traced, and no correspondence was sent. Bristol Dockyards describes its plan-ordering process on its [ship-plans page](https://www.bristoldockyards.org/ship-plans).

Niagara's geometry is unchanged in the Stonewall forecastle revision. Its hull should continue to be described as approximate pending verified hull-line references; no additional speculative correction was made in response to this screenshot.

## Confirmed archival drawing references found in the follow-up search

On 31 October 2026, downloaded the National Archives catalogue metadata and all 94 digitized cards from [Index for Plans for the Steam Frigate USS Niagara (1855), NAID 177104424](https://catalog.archives.gov/id/177104424). Local originals are in `Documentation/References/Niagara-NARA-index/`; catalogue metadata is retained in `Artifacts/niagara-1855-index.json`. These are **index cards, not the actual engineering drawings**.

Index image numbers refer to filename suffixes, e.g. `RG19_DASHINDEX_Niagara_1855_17.jpg`. Date transcriptions above were visually checked on the cards. Drawing dates do not by themselves establish the ship's final 1865 condition. Some other cards explicitly concern proposed armor conversions or much later alterations and must not be incorporated into the Ferrol reconstruction.

The corresponding [NIAGARA DASH drawing file, NAID 182787941](https://catalog.archives.gov/id/182787941), is in RG 19, Ship Design and Construction Drawings (series 564989). Its retrieved record lists physical paper sheets and no digital objects; the catalogue child-record query returned no items. This establishes a useful retrieval reference, not that scans can never be available. [NARA's ship-plan guide](https://www.archives.gov/research/cartographic/ship-plans) explains that DASH plans are located through these index cards.

**Misleading index entry excluded:** the general ship index labels NAID 53484634 as `NIAGARA / ScFr`, but the actual catalogue record is titled **Plans for the Snow-Brig USS Niagara**. Do not use it as confirmation of steam-frigate hull geometry. The steam-frigate index identified above is explicit about the correct vessel.

An additional contemporary description, [Scientific American, 1 March 1856](https://www.survivorlibrary.com/library/scientific-american-1856-03-01-v11-n25.pdf), describes a fine, sharp entrance and a round stern. This supports investigating the model's broad flat transom and abrupt corners, but supplies no offsets or precise radii. Published length conventions also differ: NHHC gives 328 ft 10 in, while the contemporary article gives an extreme length of 345 ft. Do not rescale or reshape the whole hull from those numbers without identifying their measurement endpoints.

Recommended first retrieval: 79-13-31 A, 79-13-31 J and 79-13-31 T. Use A/J for the underlying hull and T to check stern changes during the refit. Then reconcile deck and port plans with the 1863 photographs. No inquiry, purchase or external message has been sent. Geometry remains unchanged by this research-only follow-up.
