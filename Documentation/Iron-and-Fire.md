# Iron & Fire miniatures mode

Based on the user's local **Iron and Fire, Third Edition (2015), David W. Manley / A&A Game Engineering**, `contextonly/Iron_and_Fire_3rd_Edition.pdf`. The PDF and its illustrations are not bundled or redistributed. This adds manual table tools, not a full rules engine.

Choose **Scenarios / shores & forts → Iron & Fire · miniatures movement**. The host selects the ruleset for both TCP players. Either the Ferrol or Hampton Roads ships can be used. Switching back to Yaquinto Ironclads snaps both ships onto the existing 100-yard hex chart and six facings. Switching scenarios retains your chosen ruleset.

The book's ground scale (printed p.3) is one tabletop inch to 100 yards. A three-minute turn allows one inch of powered movement per current knot (p.7). The digital ruler therefore uses **91.44 meters per tabletop inch**, rather than substituting physical knot integration (which would be 92.6 meters per knot over three minutes). Models retain real-world dimensions; the oversized miniature/base footprints of a physical table are not reproduced.

In **Ships**, choose the current course and speed as explicit setup/umpire overrides, then plan straight, port or starboard segments. A green line previews the path. Choose the effective turning class: VS/S/M/L/VL use radii of 1/2/3/4/5 tabletop inches, matching the turning-circle sheet (printed p.52). The app integrates circular arcs by actual path length, keeps non-integer coordinates and headings, and tracks movement used. Use **Move remaining allowance** or multiple shorter segments to combine courses and turns. Segments cannot exceed the current ruler; the umpire may change speed or reset the ruler to authorize exceptions.

**Ordered speed** and ordered ahead/astern direction apply when the host ends the turn. Ending the turn advances the clock three minutes and resets movement rulers; it does not move ships automatically. Speed orders remain separate from current speed. The ordinary three-knot acceleration/deceleration limit, maximum/astern speeds, stopping before reversal, damage modifiers, engine forcing and turning eligibility are reminders for the umpire rather than enforced combat rules. Choose the effective turn class after the book's speed/BP modifiers. Manual ±45° pivot controls support stationary screw vessels; the umpire tracks the per-turn restriction and available power.

**Manual current / drift** applies a measured displacement toward a chosen true bearing without spending powered movement or rotating the ship. It does not calculate wind, anchoring, towing or the complete drift phase. Shift-click places the ship at the exact sea point for umpire adjustments. Movement endpoints remain inside the existing ±20 km local scene.

The gunnery ruler measures between actual positions in inches and yards, and its origin stays at the actual firing-ship center. Alt-click chooses a precise sea point. One distance band is one tabletop inch. Adjustable sectors are measuring aids: the app does not assert that a generic sector reproduces the book's weapon-specific firing templates. All grounding, line-of-sight obstruction, loading, firing eligibility, damage and results remain manual.

TCP protocol 3 synchronizes ruleset, exact position/course, current and ordered speeds/directions, effective turn class, distance used, engine orders, Monitor turret and cosmetic salvos. Ownership stays one ship per player; the host controls turn progression and ruleset/scenario selection. Both players must use the new build. Stale movement packets from a previous turn or ruleset are discarded. Saves and undo retain both sets of movement data.
