# Battery salvos, sound and powder smoke

Current player: `Builds/Windows-Salvos-Realism/Ironclads.exe`, also launched by `Start-Ironclads.ps1`. Open **Fire batteries / smoke & sound** beneath the main tabs. Choose Niagara or Stonewall, then a battery. Firing is manual and cosmetic: it never consumes ammunition, checks eligibility or damage, moves a vessel, or advances the game clock. All-guns display intentionally includes guns pointing in different directions. Repeated clicks while that ship's salvo is firing are ignored; the other ship can fire independently.

Niagara uses the selected 1865 working outfit: five 150-pounder Parrott rifles on each broadside, ten total. Stonewall has one forward 300-pounder Armstrong and two 70-pounder Armstrongs in the aft enclosure. Effect anchors are components on the actual procedural gun transforms and survive render-mesh combining. No duplicate estimated muzzle coordinates are used.

Each type now has three generated 6.5-second mono report variants containing a pressure transient, sharp crack, heavy descending boom, turbulent body and rolling tail. These are synthesized effects, not authenticated recordings of the historical guns. Audio is spatial and delayed by muzzle-to-camera distance divided by 343 m/s. A custom distance curve preserves impact at normal fleet/inspection distances; low-pass filtering softens distant reports. Pitch and ripple timing vary slightly shot to shot. The separate cannon volume/mute does not change the ocean ambience setting. Thirty-two pooled voices preserve more overlapping tails. A smooth listener-output limiter bounds combined peaks while leaving quieter samples unchanged. The original report source and level measurements are retained under `Artifacts/SalvoRealism/Baseline`; new WAV previews and body/tail RMS comparisons are in `Artifacts/SalvoRealism`. Measured level increases do not establish a particular perceived loudness on the user's speakers.

Smoke uses world-space particles, four procedural density textures in an atlas and a lit billboard shader with depth fading and subtle internal texture warping. Each gun releases three short pulses containing dense cores, large billows and thin wisps with varied size, opacity, lifetime and blast momentum. Dark gray dense pockets, as requested, blend gradually into the pale cloud; this is an artistic treatment of density/soot rather than a chemical simulation. Original shade is encoded in the particle seed and the age-based fade avoids accumulated 8-bit color rounding errors.

Initial blast momentum decays toward the scenario wind with height-dependent shear, shared spatial gusts/eddies, smaller parcel-level turbulence and varied drag/buoyancy. Wind FROM is converted to the opposite drift direction; knots convert at 0.514444 m/s. Existing particles respond to wind changes, stay in place when ships are moved, expand and fade over 20–46 real seconds. This is an atmospheric visual approximation, not computational fluid dynamics or a rules visibility calculation. The 8,000-particle cap bounds repeated-salvo cost; dense transparent smoke can increase GPU overdraw. Changing the display does not change any movement, firing or visibility ruling.

Use **Clear smoke & stop salvo / sound** to cancel queued guns and clear reports/flashes/clouds. Entering globe mode also clears battery effects. H hides the interface without clearing smoke. Volume, smoke density and ripple timing are temporary session preferences rather than save-file state.

Build with `Ironclads.Editor.ProjectBuilder.DeliverSalvoRealism`. Test with `.tools/verify-player.ps1 -DefaultGraphics -BuildDirectory 'Builds\Windows-Salvos-Realism'`. Automated salvos are muted. Tests cover actual mount/battery counts, shot counts, repeat-click rejection, live smoke advection/rise/wind reversal, spatial variation, dark/pale puff populations and size variation, restarting stopped particles, clear/cancel and unchanged scenario state. Audio checks cover non-silence, bounded samples and output mixing, inspection-distance gain, and increased body/tail RMS against the retained baseline. Automated tests do not judge subjective sound quality or verify historical acoustic fidelity. Player captures show both salvos and drift.
